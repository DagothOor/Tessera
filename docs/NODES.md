## Input

### Source (`source`)

The open texture and its alpha.

**Outputs:** image (`rgb`), A (`a`)

### Paint a mask (`paint_mask`)

A mask painted with a brush right in the 2D view. The "over the picture" output shows the texture (or whatever is connected to the input) tinted red where the mask is black, which is handy to paint on; the "mask" output is the mask itself, white = 1.

**Inputs:** picture (`rgb`, optional)

**Outputs:** over the picture (`over`), mask (`mask`)

**Parameters:**

- Start with (`base`) — black / white, black
- Feather, px (`feather`) — 0.0…100.0, 0.0
- Invert (`invert`) — False

### Treat as normal (`as_normal`)

Treat the input image as a finished normal map.

**Inputs:** image (`rgb`)

**Outputs:** normal (`n`)

**Parameters:**

- Green of the input (`green`) — auto / directx / opengl, directx
- DirectX (green down) (`directx`) — True

## Preparation

### Repair damage (`repair`)

Finds blown-out and crushed spots and fills them. What is selected is in the list above the 2D view: "mask" (white is repaired) and "mask over" (the selection tinted red). The fills "mean colour", "gradient" and "spots" take colours only from intact pixels brighter than the crush level.

**Inputs:** image (`rgb`)

**Outputs:** image (`rgb`), mask (`mask`), mask over (`overlay`)

**Parameters:**

- Blow-out level (`hi`) — 0.6…1.0, 0.9
- Crush level (`lo`) — 0.0…0.4, 0.08
- Flatness level (`flat`) — 0.005…0.12, 0.03. How much texture a spot may keep and still count as damaged. A bright or dark area with detail is just bright or dark; a damaged one is featureless. Further right takes more; near the right end only brightness decides, like a selection made with Curves.
- Mask margin, px (`grow`) — -16…16, 2. Plus grows the mask by that many pixels. Minus removes from the mask everything narrower than about twice that and leaves the rest as is: joints between stones are thin lines, a broken-out spot is wide, so −4…−8 keeps the joints and repairs the spots.
- Rim over the slope, px (`rim`) — 0…24, 6. How far the mask creeps from a found spot over its slope — the neighbouring pixels a little lighter than the crush level (or a little darker than the blow-out level). It keeps the repair from taking its sample from the slope. On dark textures the slope is the dark stone itself, and the repair runs onto it: then set 0. A negative «Mask margin» does not remove it — it only drops narrow parts such as the joints between stones.
- How much to replace (`mix`) — 0.0…1.0, 1.0
- method (`method`) — patch / tone / smooth / mean / gradient / spots, patch
- Spot seed (`seed`) — 1…99, 1. For the «spots of main colours» method: which set of spots. The same seed gives the same spots on the preview, on the refined pass and in the saved file; another number — another pattern.

### Patch (`patch`)

Manual patch, like the Patch tool in Photoshop: outline in the 2D view the place to fix and drag the outline to where the replacement should come from. Any number of patches; the texture is a tile, the shift wraps round the edge.

**Inputs:** map (`h`)

**Outputs:** map (`h`)

**Parameters:**

- Pick the source automatically (`auto`) — True. On — just outline the place: the program finds where to take the replacement from itself, where the surroundings match best (like Content-Aware in Photoshop). Off — drag the outline onto the source by hand.
- Seamless (blend the edges) (`seamless`) — True. On — the texture is carried over and the brightness and colour at the edge are smoothly brought to the surroundings (gradient-domain blend), no seam. Off — a plain copy with a feathered edge.
- Edge feather, px (`feather`) — 0.5…40.0, 4.0

### Remove light (`delight`)

Removes the smooth light gradient and hard shadows painted into the texture. The light output is what was removed.

**Inputs:** image (`rgb`)

**Outputs:** image (`rgb`), light (`light`)

**Parameters:**

- Smooth gradient (`soft`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.5
- Hard shadows (`hard`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.0
- Radius, px (`radius`) — 4…300, 48
- Keep colourfulness (`sat_keep`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.7

### Mix colour (`mix_rgb`)

Two diffuse maps as Photoshop layers: A at the bottom, B on top, amount is B's opacity, the optional mask says where B applies.

**Inputs:** A (`a`), B (`b`, optional), mask (`mask`, optional)

**Outputs:** image (`rgb`)

**Parameters:**

- mode (`mode`) — mix / multiply / screen / overlay / soft / darken / lighten, mix
- How much to blend in (`amount`) — 0.0…1.0, 1.0

### Seamless (`seamless`)

Makes a map tile by replacing a band at the edges.

**Inputs:** height (`h`)

**Outputs:** height (`h`)

**Parameters:**

- Blend width, px (`band`) — 4…128, 24

### Remove shading (`unshade`)

Takes the painted shading out of the texture: connect an AO map or the «light» output of «Shadows». The colour is divided by the shading in linear light; «floor» keeps black spots from turning into noise.

**Inputs:** image (`rgb`), shading (`shade`)

**Outputs:** image (`rgb`)

**Parameters:**

- How much to remove (`strength`) — 0.0…2.0, 1.0
- Divisor floor (`floor`) — 0.05…1.0, 0.25
- Strength automatically (`auto`) — True. The strength is found from the picture itself: how much it really darkens where the shade map says «shade» (fine brightness changes are compared, places at a change of colour are left out). «How much to remove» then multiplies it: 1 — as found.
- No halos (`no_halo`) — True. A pixel is not made brighter than the lit places of its own colour nearby (window — 3 % of the size). Removes light rims where the shade map is darker than the picture — usually at a change of colour. A large even shadow wider than the window may stay a bit dark.

### Removed light (`light_back`)

Restores the light that was taken off the picture: the original is divided by the albedo in linear light (e.g. the source and SuperMat's albedo). «light» — by brightness, «light colour» — per channel. 1 is the most lit, as in an AO map: fit for «Remove shading» and as a mask.

**Inputs:** original (`rgb`), albedo (`albedo`)

**Outputs:** light (`light`), light colour (`light_rgb`)

**Parameters:**

- Dark albedo floor (`floor`) — 0.005…0.3, 0.03. Below which albedo brightness (linear) not to divide: otherwise black spots turn into flares.
- Smoothing, px (`smooth`) — 0.0…30.0, 1.5. Blurred pictures are divided, not single pixels: albedo noise does not turn into spikes. 0 — per pixel.
- Bring to 0…1 (`norm`) — lit1 / avg05 / raw, lit1

## Filters

### High Pass (`highpass`)

Takes away everything larger than the radius: lighting gradients, blotches, overall tilt. The «removed» output shows what went.

**Inputs:** map (`h`)

**Outputs:** map (`h`), removed (`low`)

**Parameters:**

- Radius, px (`radius`) — 0.5…500.0, 40.0
- How much to remove (`remove`) — 0.0…1.0, 1.0
- Stretch to 0…1 (`stretch`) — True. On — the result is stretched to the full 0…1 range. Off — the mean level stays as in the input.
- Keep sharp edges (no halos) (`keep_edges`) — False. A plain blur spreads a sharp spot into a wide hump, and subtracting it leaves a ring around the spot — a halo. With this on, the large part is estimated keeping edges: sharp steps stay in the "large" part whole, no ring is left. Those steps are then removed too.
- An edge is a step from, levels (`edge_level`) — 1…128, 25. How large a step (of 255 levels) counts as an edge. Lower — fewer halos, but fine relief near edges goes too.

### Gaussian Blur (`lowpass`)

Plain blur; the radius is the Gaussian sigma in pixels.

**Inputs:** height (`h`)

**Outputs:** height (`h`)

**Parameters:**

- Radius, px (`radius`) — 0.3…250.0, 4.0

### Anti-aliasing (`antialias`)

Takes the staircase off edges. Algorithm to choose: FXAA, smoothing along contours, or «blur and cut again» for masks and two-colour maps.

**Inputs:** height (`h`)

**Outputs:** height (`h`)

**Parameters:**

- Algorithm (`algo`) — fxaa / contour / mask, fxaa. FXAA (NVIDIA, T. Lottes) — finds edges by brightness and resamples the pixel shifted across the edge; a fast general choice. «Along contours» — the edge direction from the structure tensor, blur only along it: the stairs go, the sharpness across stays. «Mask» — blur and cut again to a one-pixel edge: best for masks, alpha, threshold, two flat colours; details thinner than the radius fade. On detailed texture FXAA and contours touch the texture itself too — soften it.
- Amount (`amount`) — 0.0…1.0, 1.0. 0…1. For FXAA — how much single-pixel steps are softened (0.75 in the original); for the others — the share of the result.
- Radius, px (`radius`) — 0.5…4.0, 1.0. For «along contours» and «mask»: the scale of the stairs, in pixels of the full-size texture. FXAA does not use it.

### Unsharp Mask (`sharpen`)

### Bands (`bands`)

Re-weights the large and fine parts of a height.

**Inputs:** height (`h`)

**Outputs:** height (`h`)

**Parameters:**

- Scale boundary (`scale`) — 0.05…1.0, 0.3
- Large scale (`low`) — 0.0…1.5, 1.0
- Fine scale (`high`) — 0.0…2.0, 1.0

### Mix (`mix`)

Weighted sum of two heights. With "Stretch to 0…1" the result is always stretched to the full range, so a mix of dull heights comes out contrasty. Without it the inputs' contrast is kept and the sum is divided by "Divisor": with weights 1 and divisor 2 it is the plain average, which cannot leave 0…1.

**Inputs:** A (`a`, optional), B (`b`, optional)

**Outputs:** height (`h`)

**Parameters:**

- Weight of A (`wa`) — 0.0…2.0, 1.0
- Weight of B (`wb`) — 0.0…2.0, 1.0
- Stretch to 0…1 (`stretch`) — True. On — the result is stretched to the full 0…1 range. Off — the mean level stays as in the input.
- Divisor (without stretch) (`divisor`) — 0.5…8.0, 2.0

### Normal → height (`normal_to_height`)

Height from a normal map by least squares (Frankot–Chellappa). "Distrust above" removes the hill across the whole texture that builds up from an overall tilt.

**Inputs:** normal (`n`)

**Outputs:** height (`h`)

**Parameters:**

- Distrust above (`cutoff`) — 0.0…0.8, 0.0
- DirectX (green down) (`directx`) — True

### Balance normal ↔ height (`nh_balance`)

Finds one height that is at once closest to the slopes of the normal and to the given height, and a normal exactly from it. Fine detail comes from the normal, large form from the height; Balance sets where the border lies. The height scale is fitted by itself in the band where both are trusted. Keep own brings back part of the normal's slopes that no height can explain.

**Inputs:** normal (`n`), height (`h`)

**Outputs:** height (`h`), normal (`n`)

**Parameters:**

- Balance: height ← → normal (`balance`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.5. 0 — the height decides everything, 1 — the normal. In between the normal is in charge of detail finer than the border, the height of larger forms. The border in pixels is in the readouts.
- Keep the normal's own (`keep`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.0
- Boost height (`gain`) — 0.0…4.0, 1.0
- DirectX (green down) (`directx`) — True

### Repair clipped spots (plateaus) (`normal_flatfix`)

Clipped spots are flat blobs on a normal or plateaus on a height (tops and bottoms that hit the end of the range, or places where the source is blown out). Connect the node to a normal or a height: it finds such spots and covers them with relief from where the surroundings match best (like the auto patch). For a normal with «With relief» off — a smooth continuation of the tilt.

**Inputs:** map (`n`)

**Outputs:** map (`n`), mask (`mask`)

**Parameters:**

- Normal: flat if the spread is below, ° (`spread`) — 0.05…5.0, 0.5
- Height: flat if the spread is below, levels (`level`) — 0.05…5.0, 0.3
- Spot at least, px (`min_area`) — 4…5000, 30
- Take in the edge, px (`grow`) — 0…16, 3. Plus grows the mask by that many pixels. Minus removes from the mask everything narrower than about twice that and leaves the rest as is: joints between stones are thin lines, a broken-out spot is wide, so −4…−8 keeps the joints and repairs the spots.
- With relief (patch from a similar place) (`detail`) — True

### Occlusion (AO) (`ao`)

Occlusion from height. 1 is open.

**Inputs:** height (`h`), alpha (`alpha`, optional), normal (`n`, optional)

**Outputs:** AO (`ao`)

**Parameters:**

- Method (`method`) — cavity / lsao / gtao, cavity. "Hollows" — a point darkens by as much as it lies below its surroundings; does not depend on the relief depth, robust on noisy textures. "Line sweep (LSAO)" — a true horizon search along lines (Timonen, 2013): the occlusion depends on the real depth ("Relief depth") and on direction; "Radius" is the distance at which an occluder counts half when «Falloff» is on. "GTAO" (Jimenez et al., 2016) — the same horizon search, but it gives the share of diffuse light that really reaches the surface, taking its tilt into account (cosine law): a slope is not darkened just for being a slope. Picking a method sets «Strength»: 1.0 for LSAO and GTAO — the occlusion as computed, no boost (GTAO is then the physical share of light), and 0.9 for "hollows", which has no physical scale.
- Sizes in (`units`) — pct / px / cm, pct. "% of size" — radius and depth in per cent of the texture's larger side: the same project gives the same look on textures of any size. "Pixels" — in pixels of the full-size texture.
- Radius, px (`radius`) — 4…120, 28
- Radius, % of size (`radius_pct`) — 0.2…25.0, 5.5
- Radius, cm (`radius_cm`) — 0.1…200.0, 10.0
- Strength (`strength`) — 0.0…2.0, 0.9
- Relief depth, px (LSAO, GTAO) (`depth`) — 1.0…200.0, 80.0. LSAO and GTAO only: height 1 is this many pixels of the full-size texture. The deeper, the higher the horizon and the darker the hollows.
- Relief depth, % (LSAO, GTAO) (`depth_pct`) — 0.2…40.0, 15.0. LSAO and GTAO only: height 1 is this many per cent of the texture's larger side. The deeper, the higher the horizon and the darker the hollows.
- Relief depth, cm (LSAO, GTAO) (`depth_cm`) — 0.1…30.0, 7.0. LSAO and GTAO only: from the lowest place of the relief to the highest, in centimetres of the game world — as the depth in «Parallax for OpenMW». Turned into pixels through «Repeat»: how many units the texture covers along its width (22.1 units per foot).
- Repeat size (`repeat_mode`) — units / per_px, units. "In units" — one number for any texture. "Proportional to resolution" — so many units per pixel of width: a texture twice as wide covers twice as much (for a pack where resolution follows the size of the object). Not for an upscale, where the same texture just got more pixels — its repeat stays the same. Switching converts the number, so on this texture the repeat stays as it was; the modes differ only on textures of another width (in a batch).
- Repeat, units (`repeat`) — 16…1024, 128. How many game units one texture repeat covers on the model. The Morrowind and OpenMW unit: 22.1 units per foot, i.e. 1 unit = 1.38 cm = 0.54 inch; 100 units = 1.38 m = 54.3 inches. The current value converted is shown under the slider.
- Units per pixel of width (`units_px`) — 0.01…8.0, 0.5. Repeat = this number × the width of the full-size texture in pixels. 0.5 — a 256 px texture covers 128 units (1.77 m). The result is in the readouts.
- Shared depth and repeat (`relief_sync`) — False. «Parallax for OpenMW», «Relief for OpenMW» and «Occlusion (AO)» (in centimetres) nodes with this box ticked keep the depth in centimetres and the repeat size the same; parallax and relief also share the surface level. A change in one goes to the others at once. Ticking the box takes the values of a node already ticked.
- Directions (LSAO, GTAO) (`dirs`) — 4…32, 16. LSAO and GTAO only: how many directions round the circle. More is smoother but slower (time grows in proportion).
- Falloff with distance (LSAO, GTAO) (`falloff`) — False. Off — as in physics: an occluder counts at any distance, everything above the horizon blocks the sky. On — far occluders weaken, by half at the «Radius» distance (ambient obscurance): an artistic choice, not physics.
- Depth from the normal (if connected) (`n_depth`) — True. If a normal map of the same relief is connected to the «normal» input, the depth is taken from it: the height scale at which its slopes match the normal's. The AO then fits the relief the game will show through that normal, and the depth slider has no effect. Turn off to set the depth with the slider. For GTAO the tilt of each point comes from the normal either way.
- Gamma (`gamma`) — 0.2…3.0, 1.0
- Contrast (`contrast`) — 0.2…3.0, 1.0
- Alpha threshold (`alpha_cut`) — 0.01…0.99, 0.5. Where alpha is below this, it is background: not counted as a pit and left unshaded. Works when the «alpha» input is connected.
- Light from one side, share (`dir_amount`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.0. 0 — the sky lights evenly from all sides (plain AO). Higher — light comes mostly from «Light direction», and what is hidden on that side gets darker.
- Light direction, ° (`azimuth`) — 0…359, 90. 0° — from the right, 90° — from the top, 180° — from the left, 270° — from below.
- Source width, ° (`spread`) — 10…180, 60

### SSS (subsurface scattering) (`sss_map`)

Measured materials (Jensen et al., SIGGRAPH 2001; snow — France et al., 2011, its colour from the absorption of ice, Picard et al., 2016). - Scattering colour: B (`col_b`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.464

## Foliage

### Foliage (alpha card) (`foliage`)

The foliage tabs' engine as one node: the leaves are cut out of the silhouette once, and all maps come from that — per-leaf normals, height, crown AO, bent normal, translucency, and the check views «element split» and «leaves and branches». Feed the alpha from the source's «A» output, hand-marked branches into «branches by hand» (white = branch). Settings and defaults are those of the tab. The de-lit diffuse is the separate «Remove light» node.

**Inputs:** image (`rgb`), alpha (`a`), branches by hand (`hand`, optional), outside depth (`depth`, optional)

**Outputs:** normal (`n`), height (`h`), AO (`ao`), bent normal (`bent`), translucency (`sss`), translucency amount (`sss_a`), element split (`seg`), leaves and branches (`branch`)

**Parameters:**

- mode (`mode`) — leaf_silh / leaf_dense / leaf_lum, leaf_silh
- Split amount (`split`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.35. Lower values cut finer and produce more elements.
- Minimum element size, px (`min_area`) — 0…1200, 60. Pieces smaller than this are handed to their neighbours instead of living on their own. This is the cure for shattered branches: without it a long branch breaks apart at every thickening and each fragment gets its own tilt.
- Cross-section curvature (`fold`) — -1.5…1.5, 0.9. 1.0 is a true cylinder. Negative values bend the leaf the other way — a groove instead of a ridge. For cupped and concave leaves.
- Cross-section profile (`profile`) — 1.0…5.0, 2.0. 1 is a tent, 2 is round, higher gives a flat top with a sharp fall-off at the edge.
- Detail from the image (`detail`) — 0.0…0.6, 0.22. Adds fine relief taken from the texture itself: veins, serrations, bark grain.
- Random seed (`seed`) — 1…50, 3
- Bleed past the edge, px (`pad`) — 0…24, 8. The normal continues outside the silhouette. Prevents a flat rim along leaf edges after compression and in mip levels.
- Tilt (`tilt`) — 0.0…1.5, 0.45. How much each element turns its own way. This is what separates a canopy from one large crumpled leaf.
- Branch threshold (`thin`) — 1.5…12.0, 3.5. Anything more elongated than this counts as a branch: it gets no tilt, only rounding into a tube. Switch the view to «leaves and branches» to see the split: red is branch, green is leaf.
- Elongated pieces are blades (`blade`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.0. A grass blade is as elongated as a branch but does need tilt: it is a leaf in its own right, not a stem carrying others. At 1 elongated pieces turn just like the rest.
- Bend along the element (`bend`) — -1.0…1.0, 0.1. An arc along the length of a piece: one end turns away from the light, the other towards it. Blades and twigs are never straight, and without this they read as sticks.
- Clumps (`clump_mid`) — -1.5…1.5, 0.5. The middle scale: neither the individual leaf nor the whole card, but the volume of clumps. Derived from foliage density — thicker mass sits higher. Negative values make clumps concave.
- Large masses (`clump_big`) — -1.5…1.5, 0.25. Four times the clump scale.
- Clump size, px (`clump_scale`) — 6…90, 24
- Convexity across width (`dome_w`) — -1.2…1.2, 0.0. A dome over the whole card, sized to the silhouette. Both equal gives a sphere, one alone gives a cylinder. Negative values make the card concave — for the inside of a canopy, or for recesses.
- Convexity across height (`dome_h`) — -1.2…1.2, 0.0. A dome over the whole card, sized to the silhouette. Both equal gives a sphere, one alone gives a cylinder. Negative values make the card concave — for the inside of a canopy, or for recesses.
- DirectX (green down) (`directx`) — True
- Height (`h_mode`) — depth / normal / shape, depth. How the «height» output is made. Crown depth — every leaf at its own random depth (the same as for the AO and translucency; spread is «Leaf depth spread» on the AO tab) plus its bulge; steps between leaves. From the normal — the part of the normal that is a surface: the volume of clumps and of the card, smooth, no steps; the tilts of single leaves do not get into it. By piece shape — as before: every leaf a ridge from its edge to the middle.
- Trust in the outside depth (`depth_w`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.5. Works when something is connected to the «outside depth» input (light = closer). Each leaf takes its depth from it — the mean over the leaf — instead of a random one. 0 — the order stays random; 1 — entirely from the outside depth. The middle is usually right: neighbouring leaves often look equal to the outside depth, and without the scatter they fall into one plane again. This depth feeds the height, the AO and the translucency.
- AO strength (`ao_str`) — 0.0…1.5, 1.0
- Leaf depth spread, px (`ao_spread`) — 0…48, 12. A card has no depth, and one thing shades another only when it lies above it. So depth is invented: every leaf from the split gets its own random offset. The seed is the same as for the tilts on the normals tab, so depth and normal describe one and the same twig. More spread means stronger contact shadows where one leaf tucks behind another.
- Leaf bulge, px (`ao_bulge`) — 0…30, 6. The leaf's own volume within that invented depth, from edge to middle. It produces the soft shadow along the margin where a leaf rests on its neighbour.
- Sweep radius, px (`ao_radius`) — 4…80, 24. How far the horizon is searched. Roughly one leaf: beyond that, neighbours block too small a part of the sky to matter.
- Account for the leaf's tilt (`ao_tilt`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.4. The horizon is measured not from the plane of the card but from the plane of the leaf itself, taken from the normal map: a tilted leaf hides part of the sky with its own body. This is the honest form of «occlusion from tilt» — as a separate map it was merely the blue channel of the normal, but inside a horizon sweep it belongs. Above 0.5 a leaf starts shading itself more than its neighbours shade it.
- AO: gamma (`ao_gamma`) — 0.2…3.0, 1.0
- AO: contrast (`ao_contrast`) — 0.2…3.0, 1.0
- Translucency strength (`sss_str`) — 0.0…2.0, 1.0
- Leaf blade (`sss_base`) — 0.0…1.5, 0.15. The constant thickness of the blade itself, the same across the whole leaf. On a real leaf it genuinely is nearly constant: there is no glowing rim around the edge in nature.
- Veins (`sss_veins`) — 0.0…2.5, 0.9. Veins and margins are thickenings and pass no light. Backlit they show as a dark network over a glowing blade, and that is the first thing by which the eye recognises a leaf against the sun.
- Leaves behind (`sss_behind`) — 0.0…2.0, 1.0. The main thing you see backlit: through the front blade shows the dark silhouette of another leaf — the one that blocked the sun. It is not painted on the card, being hidden, but the depth already exists and continuing it under its neighbour is easy: if a leaf sitting distinctly lower is nearby, it passes beneath us. Light direction plays no part, so this bakes into the map: what lies behind a leaf lies behind it under any sun.
- How far leaves reach behind, px (`sss_reach`) — 2…60, 14. How far a leaf continues underneath its neighbour. About half a leaf width: more and the silhouettes behind spread out, less and they stay a narrow band along the edge.
- Foliage layers (`sss_layers`) — 0.0…2.5, 0.5. How much foliage lies on top. It uses the same occlusion as the AO map, so the darkening takes the shape of the neighbouring leaves rather than a soft blob.
- Pigment density (`sss_pigment`) — 0.0…2.0, 0.7. A darker leaf holds more pigment and passes less light. It is also exactly how background leaves are painted on a texture — darker than the ones in front.
- Thinner at the edge (`sss_edge`) — 0.0…1.5, 0.15. A small addition right at the margin. Keep it small: make thickness depend mainly on distance from the edge and you get a glowing outline instead of a glowing leaf.
- Branches are opaque (`sss_branch`) — 0.0…1.0, 1.0. Branches and trunks pass no light, and that has to be stated explicitly: by silhouette alone a thick branch is no different from a thick leaf. Uses the same branches as the first tab, hand-marked ones included.
- Filter saturation (`sss_tint`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.35. Transmitted light travelled through the pigment for longer than reflected light, so its hue is always purer: green greener, autumn yellower. At 0 the transmitted colour matches the leaf itself.
- Translucency: gamma (`sss_gamma`) — 0.2…3.0, 1.0
- Translucency: contrast (`sss_contrast`) — 0.2…3.0, 1.0

- Material from the texture name (`auto_name`) — False. If the file name names a material (tx_wood_…, tx_dwrv_…, …_rust…), its values are used, with several — the range from the dullest to the shiniest of them; otherwise the sliders. What was found is in the readouts. Works in a batch too.
- Cut dull, % of pixels (`clip_dull`) — 0.0…40.0, 2.0. This share of the roughest pixels is left out when the spread of the roughness map is found: they just get the dullest value. Useful when the dullest thing is a background or junk that does not go on the model — else it squeezes the rest of the texture to the shiny end.
- Cut shiny, % of pixels (`clip_shiny`) — 0.0…40.0, 2.0. This share of the smoothest pixels is left out when the spread of the roughness map is found: they get the shiniest value. Useful when the shiniest thing is a background that does not go on the model — else the main texture shines less than it should.
- F0 of the dull end (head-on reflection) (`f0`) — 0.0…0.2, 0.04. Share of light reflected head-on, linear. For dielectrics it follows from the refractive index: water 0.02, glass and varnish 0.04, stone 0.045, gems 0.075. Metals take the specular colour from the base colour.
- F0 of the shiny end (`f0_shiny`) — 0.0…0.2, 0.04
- Roughness at the shiny end (`rough_lo`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.0
- Roughness at the dull end (`rough_hi`) — 0.0…1.0, 1.0
- Metalness from (`metal_lo`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.0
- Metalness to (`metal_hi`) — 0.0…1.0, 1.0
- Lay maps into the materials (`fit`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.0. 1 — the spread of the incoming maps is stretched onto the materials' range (the pattern stays, the levels change); 0 — the maps as they are.
- Roughness without a map (`rough_default`) — 0.05…1.0, 0.7
- Metalness without a map (`metal_default`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.0
- Darken metal (`metal_dark`) — 0.0…1.0, 0.7
- AO into diffuse (`ao_diffuse`) — 0.0…2.0, 1.0
- AO into specular (`ao_spec`) — 0.0…2.0, 1.0
- Specular strength (`spec_gain`) — 0.0…3.0, 1.0. A manual multiplier on top of the physics. The material's specular strength is already set by F0 and roughness, so picking a material resets it to 1; below 1 dims the highlight to taste.
- Roughness dims the reflection (`env_rough`) — 0.0…1.0, 1.0. For the «reflection mask» output: a Morrowind environment map cannot blur, and a rough surface with a sharp mirror image would look like chrome. 1 — the reflection fades as (1 − roughness)², 0 — not at all (physically the reflected share does not depend on roughness, it only spreads).

### Output by channels (`out_pack`)

A file whose R, G, B and (optionally) A channels are taken from different maps. For each channel you pick what of the connected map goes into it: its brightness or one of its channels. An unconnected R, G or B is filled with a number; without A the file has no alpha of its own.

**Inputs:** R (`r`, optional), G (`g`, optional), B (`b`, optional), A (`a`, optional)

**Outputs:** map (`any`)
